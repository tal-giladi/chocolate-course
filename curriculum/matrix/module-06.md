## Statements without a direct source (judgement or general practice)

- Truffle ratios for milk 2.5:1 and white 3:1: common practice, consistent in direction with Valrhona and Callebaut soft-ganache ratios; no reputable page opened that states them. Lessons call them "נקודת התחלה שלנו".
- Pourable ratios 1:1 / 1.5:1 / 2:1: general practice.
- Split-demo quantities (30 g 70% + 8 g cream reliably splits): derived from King Arthur's 33% limit; not tested.
- Re-weighing cream after infusion and infusing with ~50% extra cream; 10-minute covered infusion: general practice.
- Alcohol up to ~10% of ganache weight, added at 35-40 °C, replacing the same weight of cream: our starting point. "Alcohol evaporates in hot cream" is general chemistry, not sourced.
- Tahini up to ~20% of chocolate weight: our limit, not sourced.
- Invert sugar binds more water per gram because its molecules are smaller: standard colligative reasoning; no source opened says it for ganache.
- Truffle size 11-12 g: derived from Callebaut (~550 g → ~50 pieces).
- Fridge 2-3 h instead of 12-36 h crystallisation is enough for rolling: practice, not sourced.
- Cracked shells from frozen centres (expansion on warming): general practice.
- Powdered-sugar coating dissolving in the fridge was not used.
- Home shelf-life rule "up to ~2 weeks in the fridge" relies on Keylink's blog plus KSU measurements; it is guidance, not a validated shelf-life study.
- "Long-life" practice ganache (100/40/10/10) aw not measured; lesson says so.
- Valrhona "6D" next to 2-4 °C on the classic dessert ganache: meaning unclear (probably days); not used.
