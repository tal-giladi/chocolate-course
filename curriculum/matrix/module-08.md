## Statements without a direct source (judgement or general practice)

- Target shell thickness 2-3 mm for a 5-6 cm hollow half; "two layers are enough" for 5-6 cm (derived from Callebaut "bigger = thicker / repeat" and Le Cordon Bleu's two layers on a large egg).
- Home cooling: 10 min room + 15-20 min home fridge with openings up (adapted from Callebaut 14-16 °C / 30 min).
- Warm pan "on lowest heat 20-30 s then off, warm to the back of the hand"; 1-2 s per half: practical adaptation of Callebaut/Food Network wording ("a few seconds", "just a second").
- All weights (half-sphere 12-15 g, dreidel parts 10-12 / 5-6 / 1.5-2 g, dreidel 18-22 g, box ~100-110 g): computed from volume × ~1.3 g/cm³; lesson says weigh your own.
- Dreidel-from-parts method (square shells → cube, baking-paper cone tip, piped stem, mirror-written letters on acetate, glue dot used when edges dull): my design from standard techniques; the "glue when edges dull" timing borrows Le Cordon Bleu's stand technique.
- Couverture cost "~80-100 ₪/kg" summarised from where-to-buy.md prices of 2026-11-10 (Callebaut 500 g 48.90 → ~98 ₪/kg; Lubeka 1 kg 79.90-89.90).
- Dreidel size "usually 4-6 cm" in boxes: general observation, no source.
- Hot milk "until steaming, not boiling"; mug ≥300 ml for 200 ml; 1-2 min wait before a child drinks: safety judgement (NHS/Beterem support the general risk, not these numbers).
- Hot chocolate bomb shell 25-30 g + 10 g chopped chocolate ≈ 35 g (combines Callebaut dose with computed shell weight).
- Sprinkles as choking hazard under age 4: general child-safety practice, not sourced.
- Kashrut: whether a self-made silicone/plastic mould needs טבילה — not ruled on; lesson says ask the rabbi.
