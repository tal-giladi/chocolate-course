## Statements without a direct source (judgement or general practice)

- "Water weight × 1.2 ≈ chocolate weight" for a cavity: chocolate density ~1.2-1.3 g/ml, general knowledge, not from an opened page.
- Seeing detachment from below through a transparent polycarbonate mould: common practitioner observation, not in an opened source.
- Silicone releases by peeling and therefore hides a failed temper: reasoning from Callebaut's contraction explanation; Hill Country only says silicone is matte and easy to demould.
- Home cooling times (15-20 min room, up to 15-20 min fridge) and filling set time 20-30 min: adapted from module 3 (CakeFlix) and Callebaut's professional 14-16 °C; not a manufacturer number for home fridges.
- Home bar shell 2-3 mm and "hold against light, add a second layer": judgement; Callebaut only says too thin / too thick and 2-3 layers.
- Transfer mechanism (printed cocoa butter melts slightly into the chocolate and leaves with it on contraction): explanation built from Hill Country ("too hot melts the printed cocoa butter") and the contraction principle.
- Weight on thin chocolate to stop curling: general practice (seen in search snippets of ckbk/pastry books, not opened).
- Stick embedded 2-3 cm, twist half a turn: general practice.
- Coin 3-4 cm × 3 mm ≈ 4 g: from the Pelechef polycarbonate coin mould listing in `references/where-to-buy.md` (14 × 4 g).
- Lollipop/child safety sentence (supervise, no running with stick): general safety advice, no source opened.
- Feuilletine often contains butter/milk; carmine E120 in red transfer-sheet colours: general knowledge; lessons say "check the package".
