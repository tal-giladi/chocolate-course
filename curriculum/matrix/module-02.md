## Statements without a direct source (judgement or general practice)

- Compound coating melting ceiling "no more than 45 °C if the pack says nothing": our safety rule, not a manufacturer number (no compound melting temperature found). Lessons say so.
- Compound coating behaviour in a hot room ("depends on the product"): not sourced; 02.3 asks learners to test and note.
- Cold fruit causes condensation on the fruit, cracking/peeling of the coating: physics/general practice; Food Republic covers wet fruit only.
- Strawberries keep leaking juice under the coating after some hours: general practice.
- Dried fruit dipped in compound "keeps like the dried fruit itself": reasoning from low free water; no shelf-life number given.
- Nut roasting 160-170 °C for 8-10 min: general practice.
- ~8 g per 4-5 cm mendiant, bark 3-5 mm, 3x3x3 cm cube ≈ 35 g, stick 1.5-2 cm deep, 2-3 g cocoa per stick, spoon ≈ 10 g: our estimates.
- Compound in hot chocolate "tastes of compound": judgement.
- Strawberry insect checking for kashrut: lessons only say some people check or buy supervised produce, ask the rabbi; no source opened.
- Plant milks (oat thicker, almond thinner) for hot chocolate: general practice.
